## 1. Overview

A **Course** represents a structured learning or training program offered by an Organization on Tutorog.

A course defines **what is being taught**, while a Batch defines **when, where, and to whom it is being taught**.

For example:

> **Course:** Mathematics Foundation
> **Batch:** Class 10 — Evening Batch, Mon/Wed/Fri at 5 PM

A single course can have multiple batches, allowing an organization to offer the same program to different groups of students.

---

## 2. Purpose of Courses

Courses provide a common structure for:

* Describing an organization's offerings.
* Helping visitors discover learning opportunities.
* Organizing students' learning.
* Connecting tutors and batches.
* Sharing learning materials.
* Tracking progress.
* Managing course-related fees.
* Presenting consistent information to prospective students.

The course is therefore one of the central entities connecting **discovery, enrollment, and learning**.

---

## 3. Course Lifecycle

A course generally follows this lifecycle:

```text
Draft
  ↓
Published
  ↓
Active
  ↓
Archived
```

### Draft

The organization is creating or preparing the course.

It should not normally be visible to the public.

### Published

The course is available for discovery and enrollment, depending on the organization's enrollment settings.

### Active

The course is currently being delivered through one or more active batches.

### Archived

The organization no longer offers the course but may want to retain its historical data.

Archived courses should generally not accept new enrollments.

---

## 4. Course Information

A course should contain enough information for both **prospective learners** and **existing students**.

### Basic Information

* Course name
* Description
* Category
* Subject
* Cover/image
* Learning objectives
* Course level
* Target age/learner group

### Course Structure

* Duration
* Topics/modules
* Learning outcomes
* Course materials
* Assignments
* Assessments where applicable

### Commercial Information

* Course fee
* Fee structure
* Discounts where applicable
* Payment terms

### Discovery Information

* Organization
* Available batches
* Location
* Delivery mode
* Tutor/trainer
* Ratings/reviews where applicable

---

## 5. Course vs Batch

This distinction is fundamental to Tutorog.

### Course

Defines:

> **What are we teaching?**

### Batch

Defines:

> **When and to whom are we teaching it?**

For example:

```text
Course
Mathematics Foundation
│
├── Batch A
│   ├── Mon/Wed/Fri
│   ├── 5:00 PM
│   ├── Tutor: Amit
│   └── 20 students
│
├── Batch B
│   ├── Tue/Thu/Sat
│   ├── 6:00 PM
│   ├── Tutor: Neha
│   └── 18 students
│
└── Batch C
    ├── Weekend
    ├── 10:00 AM
    ├── Tutor: Amit
    └── 15 students
```

The same course content and learning objectives can therefore be reused across multiple batches.

---

## 6. Course Ownership

Every course belongs to an **Organization**.

```text
Organization
    │
    ├── Course
    │     ├── Batch
    │     ├── Batch
    │     └── Batch
    │
    └── Course
          ├── Batch
          └── Batch
```

The Organization Owner is responsible for creating and managing courses.

Tutors may contribute to course content depending on their permissions, but they should not automatically have organization-wide course management access.

---

## 7. Categories & Subjects

Courses should be connected to Tutorog's platform-managed taxonomy.

For example:

```text
Category: Academics
    ↓
Subject: Mathematics
    ↓
Course: Mathematics Foundation
```

Or:

```text
Category: Dance
    ↓
Subject: Classical Dance
    ↓
Course: Kathak Beginners
```

This allows Tutorog to maintain a consistent discovery system across organizations.

Organizations should be able to describe their courses freely, while the underlying **categories and subjects remain standardized** by the platform.

## 8. Course Discovery

Courses are an important part of the public Tutorog experience.

A prospective student or parent should be able to discover courses through:

* Category pages
* Subject pages
* Organization profiles
* Search
* Location-based discovery
* Recommendations
* Shared course links

A course listing should provide enough information to decide whether to explore further.

Example:

```text
Mathematics Foundation

Academics · Mathematics

For Classes 9–10
Duration: 6 months
Starting from ₹12,000

★ 4.8

3 batches available

[ View Course ]
```

## 9. Course Detail Page

The course detail page should answer the visitor's most important questions.

### What is this?

Course name and description.

### Who is it for?

Age, grade, skill level, or eligibility.

### What will I learn?

Topics and learning outcomes.

### Who teaches it?

Tutor/trainer information where appropriate.

### When is it offered?

Available batches and schedules.

### How much does it cost?

Fee information.

### Who offers it?

Organization information and reputation.

### How do I join?

Enrollment or enquiry options.

## 10. Course & Learning Content

A course can provide the structure around learning content.

For example:

```text
Mathematics Foundation
│
├── Module 1 — Number Systems
│   ├── Notes
│   ├── Worksheet
│   └── Assignment
│
├── Module 2 — Algebra
│   ├── Notes
│   ├── Video
│   └── Assignment
│
└── Module 3 — Geometry
    ├── Notes
    └── Assignment
```

This structure allows the same course framework to be used by multiple batches.

However, the actual delivery of content may be customized by the tutor or batch.

---

## 11. Course Enrollment

A course can be associated with one or more enrollment paths.

### Organization-led

The student contacts or visits the organization and the organization creates the enrollment.

### Tutorog-led

The student or parent discovers the course on Tutorog and requests enrollment.

### Direct enrollment

The organization may allow a student to directly enroll in an available batch.

The course itself should not necessarily determine the enrollment workflow. This can be configured at the organization/course level.

---

## 12. Course Fees

A course may have a default fee, but the actual fee charged can depend on the organization and enrollment arrangement.

For example:

```text
Course Fee
₹12,000

Possible variations:
├── Monthly payment
├── Full payment
├── Discounted enrollment
└── Batch-specific pricing
```

This suggests that the Course should provide a **default commercial definition**, while actual financial records should belong to the student's enrollment/payment relationship.

This prevents historical payment records from changing when the course's current price changes.

---

## 13. Course Materials & Assignments

Course-level resources can provide reusable content.

For example:

* Standard course notes.
* Reference materials.
* Worksheets.
* Videos.
* Course assignments.

However, tutors may also need to provide **batch-specific or student-specific material**.

Therefore:

```text
Course Material
      │
      ├── Available to all batches
      │
      └── Reusable course content

Batch Material
      │
      └── Specific to a particular class/group
```

This separation gives organizations flexibility without duplicating course structures.

---

## 14. Course Progress

Course progress represents how much of a course a student has completed.

It may eventually include:

* Modules completed.
* Topics completed.
* Assignments completed.
* Assessments completed.
* Skills achieved.
* Overall completion percentage.

For example:

```text
Mathematics Foundation

Course Progress: 72%

✓ Number Systems
✓ Algebra
✓ Linear Equations
→ Geometry
○ Statistics
○ Probability
```

Progress belongs to the **student's learning relationship with the course**, rather than to the course itself.

---

## 15. Course Management by Organization

The Organization Owner should be able to:

* Create courses.
* Edit course information.
* Publish/unpublish courses.
* Archive courses.
* Manage course pricing.
* Add learning content.
* Create batches for the course.
* View enrolled students.
* View course-level reports.

The owner should also be able to see whether a course is:

* Not yet offered.
* Currently active.
* Full.
* Temporarily unavailable.
* Archived.

---

## 16. Course Management by Tutor

Tutors should generally have limited course access.

Depending on organization permissions, a tutor may be able to:

* View course information.
* Add learning materials.
* Create assignments.
* Update learning content.
* Track student progress.

They should not automatically be able to:

* Delete courses.
* Change organization-wide pricing.
* Publish/unpublish courses.
* Modify organization settings.
* Access unrelated courses.

---

## 17. Course Visibility

A course may have different visibility states:

```text
Draft
  ↓
Private
  ↓
Public
  ↓
Archived
```

### Draft

Only organization staff can see it.

### Private

The course is available to selected students or through a direct link, but not general discovery.

### Public

The course appears in Tutorog discovery and the organization's public page.

### Archived

The course is no longer offered to new students but historical enrollments and learning records remain intact.

The exact visibility model can be refined as the discovery system evolves.

## 18. Course Reports

The Organization Owner should eventually be able to understand how each course performs.

Useful metrics include:

### Enrollment

* Total enrollments.
* Active students.
* New enrollments.
* Withdrawals.

### Learning

* Course completion.
* Assignment completion.
* Assessment performance.
* Student progress.

### Attendance

* Average attendance.
* Students with low attendance.

### Financial

* Course revenue.
* Collected fees.
* Outstanding fees.

### Reputation

* Course ratings.
* Student/parent reviews.

## 19. Important Product Principles

### 1. Course ≠ Batch

A course defines the learning offering. A batch defines its scheduled delivery.

### 2. Course ≠ Enrollment

A course is an offering; an enrollment represents a particular student's participation in that offering.

### 3. Course ≠ Payment

Course pricing describes what an organization charges. Actual payments belong to the student's financial relationship with the organization.

### 4. Course ≠ Student Progress

The course defines the learning structure. Progress belongs to the individual student's learning journey.

### 5. Course content should be reusable

A course should ideally be reusable across multiple batches without duplicating the entire course structure.

## 20. Core Relationship

The overall relationship can be understood as:

```text
Organization
     │
     │ offers
     ▼
   Course
     │
     │ delivered through
     ▼
   Batch
     │
     │ contains
     ▼
Enrollment
     │
     │ belongs to
     ▼
 Student
```

With tutors participating in the delivery:

```text
                 Organization
                      │
                      ▼
                    Course
                      │
             ┌────────┴────────┐
             ▼                 ▼
           Batch             Batch
             │                 │
          Tutor             Tutor
             │                 │
             └────────┬────────┘
                      ▼
                  Students
```

This model keeps the product conceptually clean:

> **Organizations offer Courses. Courses are delivered through Batches. Tutors teach Batches. Students enroll in Batches and progress through Courses.**
